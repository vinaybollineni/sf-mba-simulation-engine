ENT 402 — Entrepreneurship Foundations

Quarter 4 · Year 1 · 10 Weeks · Apr 5 – Jun 11, 2027

ENT 402

Entrepreneurship Foundations

Upcoming — Quarter 4 · Year 1
View Curriculum →
Professor's Welcome Note:

Every year, a handful of Okta engineers leave to start companies. Most of them fail within 18 months — not because the technology is bad, but because they've never had to sell, raise money, or find product-market fit. This course is the antidote. We'll apply the same rigor you bring to system design to the problem of building a company. By Week 10, you'll have your first investor pitch deck — and you'll know exactly what questions a Series A investor will ask before you walk in the door.

Learning Objectives

  • Apply lean startup methodology to validate ideas before building
  • Develop and stress-test a Business Model Canvas for a real startup idea
  • Conduct 10+ customer discovery interviews using the Jobs-to-be-Done framework
  • Build a credible investor pitch deck and present it confidently
  • Understand the economics of startup fundraising: pre-seed, seed, and Series A

Weekly Syllabus

Week 1 · Active Now
Founder Mindset & Opportunity Recognition
Open →
Topics:
  • What makes a good startup idea vs. a bad one
  • Peter Thiel's 0-to-1 framework
  • Why tech-to-exec transitions create unique startup advantages
  • The founder's edge: domain expertise as unfair advantage
Deliverables:
  • Write your startup thesis (1 page, due Fri Apr 9)
  • Watch YC Startup School Module 1 (due Sun Apr 11)
Week 2
Customer Discovery — Jobs-to-be-Done Framework
Locked
Christensen's Jobs-to-be-Done theory in practice. How to conduct a customer discovery interview that actually reveals the truth — and why most founders ask the wrong questions. What you're really listening for when customers describe their problems. Three real interviews due this week.
Week 3
Business Model Canvas
Locked
Osterwalder's Business Model Canvas — the single most useful one-page tool in entrepreneurship. Value propositions, customer segments, channels, revenue streams, and key partners. Build your first complete BMC for your startup idea and identify the riskiest assumptions.
Week 4
Lean MVP — Build, Measure, Learn
Locked
Minimum viable product, fake-door tests, and landing page MVPs. How Dropbox validated with a video, Airbnb validated by photographing apartments themselves, and Zappos validated by selling shoes they didn't own. Design your own validation experiment without writing a line of code.
Week 5 · Mid-Project Due
Market Sizing — TAM, SAM, SOM
Locked
Bottom-up vs. top-down market sizing — and why investors can tell the difference immediately. Why your TAM needs to be big enough for a venture return but credible enough to survive due diligence. Mid-project due: full market size analysis for your startup idea, with a bottom-up model.
Week 6
Unit Economics — CAC, LTV, Payback Period
Locked
Customer acquisition cost, lifetime value, and the payback period that separates efficient growth from a burning building. How SaaS unit economics differ from consumer, and what "good" looks like for identity-adjacent B2B SaaS startups competing in Okta's orbit.
Week 7
Competitive Positioning for Startups
Locked
Differentiation strategy for new entrants — how to carve out defensible space when incumbents have more resources. Defensible moats at early stage: proprietary data, unique distribution, and speed. How to compete against Microsoft Entra ID without being crushed.
Week 8
Fundraising Basics — Angels, Pre-seed, Seed
Locked
Funding stages, typical check sizes, and what angels look for vs. VCs. SAFE notes and convertible notes — how they work and why they matter. The venture math that drives VC decisions: why 20x returns are the target and what that means for your raise strategy.
Week 9
Pitch Deck Construction
Locked
The Sequoia Capital pitch deck structure — every slide, in order, with what each one must accomplish. What VCs actually read vs. skim. Storytelling vs. data density. How to handle the "why you?" slide when you're a first-time founder with a technical background.
Week 10 · FINAL PROJECT DUE
Pitch Presentation
Locked
Final Pitch: Deliver your 10-minute investor pitch as if presenting to real Series A investors. Final deliverable: complete deck + financial model summary + 2-page executive summary. Due: June 11, 2027.
