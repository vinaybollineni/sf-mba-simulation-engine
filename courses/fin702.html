FIN 702 — Startup Finance, Fundraising & Venture Capital

Quarter 7 · Year 2 · 10 Weeks · Feb 4 – Apr 9, 2028

FIN 702

Startup Finance, Fundraising & Venture Capital

Upcoming
Go to Week 1 Dashboard →
Professor's Welcome Note:

You've built the skills, the plan, and the team. Now it's time to talk about money. FIN 702 is not corporate finance — it's founder finance. How does a VC fund make money? How do term sheets actually work? What's the difference between a valuation cap and a discount? And when your runway is 4 months, what are your real options? By Week 10, you'll have the complete fundraising package for your startup — ready to send to investors.

Learning Objectives

  • Understand VC fund mechanics: fund structure, carry, management fees, GP/LP dynamics
  • Value pre-revenue startups using comparable transactions, Berkus method, and scorecard methods
  • Negotiate term sheets: pre/post-money, liquidation preferences, anti-dilution, drag-along rights
  • Build a complete 3-year startup financial model with monthly granularity
  • Manage cash, burn rate, and runway through a fundraising process

VC Fund Mechanics — How VCs Make Money

Deliverables:

Week 2
Startup Valuation — Pre-money, Post-money, Dilution
Locked
Berkus method; scorecard valuation; comparable transactions; why pre-revenue valuations are more art than science; how Okta was valued at its Series A.
Week 3
Term Sheet Deep Dive
Locked
Liquidation preferences (1x non-participating vs. participating); anti-dilution protection (broad-based WA vs. full ratchet); pro-rata rights; board composition; information rights; what to negotiate and what to accept.
Week 4
Building a 3-Year Startup Financial Model
Locked
Revenue model (ARR, expansion, churn); headcount plan; COGS structure for SaaS; how to build assumptions that are defensible to investors; gross margin benchmarks for identity SaaS.
Week 5 · Mid-Course Project Due
Unit Economics Deep Dive
Locked
CAC payback period; LTV:CAC ratio; magic number; Rule of 40; how to present unit economics when you have limited data. Mid-project: Complete financial model for your startup (12-month monthly, Year 2-3 annual).
Week 6
Fundraising Strategy — Story, Deck, Data Room
Locked
The narrative arc of a great fundraise; how to structure a 15-slide deck; what goes in a Series A data room; how to manage multiple VCs simultaneously; warm intro strategies.
Week 7
Managing Cash — Burn Rate, Runway, Milestones
Locked
Default alive vs. default dead (Paul Graham); how to extend runway without raising (cut vs. grow decisions); the "Series A crunch" and how to avoid it.
Week 8
Down Rounds, Bridges & Difficult Capital Situations
Locked
When your valuation doesn't hold up; inside rounds; bridge notes; pay-to-play provisions; communicating difficult financial situations to your board.
Week 9 · Case: Okta IPO
Exit Strategy — M&A vs. IPO
Locked
When to sell vs. when to go public; how acquirers value tech companies; the IPO process for a SaaS company; secondary markets and liquidity; how Okta's IPO was structured.
Week 10 · FINAL PROJECT DUE
Full Fundraising Package
Locked
Final Project: 15-slide Series A deck, 3-year financial model, 2-page executive summary, data room checklist, investor target list with thesis for each firm. Due: April 9, 2028.
