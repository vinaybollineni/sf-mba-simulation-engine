Quarter 1 · Year 1 · 10 Weeks · May 4 – July 10, 2026

ACCT 101

Financial Accounting & Analysis

Active — Week 1 of 10
Go to Week 1 Dashboard →
Professor's Welcome Note:

Financial accounting is the language of business. As an engineering manager, you already understand systems — accounting is just another system, with strict rules and invariants. By the end of this course, you'll be able to pick up any company's annual report and understand its financial health, competitive position, and how management is allocating capital. This is a foundational skill for every executive and entrepreneur.

Learning Objectives

  • Read, interpret, and critically analyze income statements, balance sheets, and cash flow statements
  • Understand GAAP principles and how accounting choices affect reported results
  • Calculate and interpret 15+ financial ratios across liquidity, profitability, leverage, and efficiency
  • Understand SaaS-specific accounting: deferred revenue, ARR, RPO, stock-based compensation
  • Build a three-statement financial model from scratch in Excel
  • Write an executive-level financial analysis memo

Deliverables:

Week 2
Income Statement — Revenue, COGS & Expenses
Locked
Revenue recognition principles (ASC 606), COGS vs. operating expenses, gross margin, operating income, EBITDA. SaaS revenue recognition: why subscription revenue is recognized ratably. Case: Analyze Okta's income statement.
Week 3
Balance Sheet Deep Dive
Locked
Current vs. non-current assets, working capital, intangible assets, goodwill, deferred revenue, stockholders' equity components. Special focus on SaaS deferred revenue and its strategic implications.
Week 4
Cash Flow Statement
Locked
Operating, investing, and financing activities. Direct vs. indirect method. Free cash flow (FCF) calculation. Why cash flow ≠ net income (and why investors care about FCF). Okta's FCF profile and what it means.
Week 5 · Mid-Course Project Due
Financial Ratios & Performance Analysis
Locked
Liquidity ratios (current, quick), profitability ratios (ROE, ROA, gross margin), leverage ratios (D/E, interest coverage), efficiency ratios (asset turnover). 3-Statement Model project due. Build a full model for a startup from scratch.
Week 6
Working Capital Management
Locked
Cash conversion cycle, accounts receivable management, inventory (and why SaaS has none), accounts payable strategy. How negative working capital is a competitive moat for SaaS businesses.
Week 7
Cost Accounting & Managerial Decisions
Locked
Fixed vs. variable costs, contribution margin, break-even analysis, cost-volume-profit relationships. How engineering managers can use cost accounting to make better resource allocation decisions.
Week 8
Depreciation, Amortization & Intangible Assets
Locked
Straight-line vs. accelerated depreciation, amortization of intangibles, goodwill impairment, capitalized software development costs (directly relevant to Okta). How D&A affects P&L vs. cash flow.
Week 9 · Case: Netflix
Putting It All Together — Integrated Case Analysis
Locked
Full financial statement analysis of Netflix's transformation. Analyze all three statements together. Identify red flags, quality of earnings, and the real story behind the numbers. Prepare for your final project.
Week 10 · FINAL PROJECT DUE
Okta 10-K — Comprehensive Financial Analysis
Locked
Final Project: Complete financial analysis of Okta's most recent 10-K. Cover: 3 statements, 5 key ratios, trend analysis (3-year), SaaS metrics (ARR, NRR, CAC), competitive benchmarking vs. CrowdStrike & Zscaler, and an executive summary memo (2 pages). Due: July 10, 2026.
