ETH 303 — Business Ethics, Leadership & Corporate Governance

Quarter 3 · Year 1 · 8 Weeks · Feb 9 – Apr 2, 2027

ETH 303

Business Ethics, Leadership & Corporate Governance

Upcoming — Starts Feb 9, 2027
Go to Week 1 →
Professor's Welcome Note:

Ethics isn't compliance — it's judgment. As you move toward VP and CTO roles, you'll face situations where the law says one thing and your values say another. Identity and security is one of the most ethically loaded domains in tech: you're building the systems that decide who gets access to what, for billions of people. This course will give you the frameworks to make hard calls under pressure — and to build organizations that don't need you in the room to do the right thing.

Learning Objectives

  • Apply ethical frameworks (utilitarian, deontological, virtue ethics) to real business dilemmas
  • Understand corporate governance structures: board composition, fiduciary duty, audit committees
  • Navigate stakeholder conflicts and growing ESG pressures on tech companies
  • Analyze ethics in tech: data privacy, AI bias, surveillance capitalism, responsible AI deployment
  • Build an ethical leadership framework for founding or leading a company

Weekly Syllabus

Week 1 · Active Now
Ethical Frameworks — Utilitarianism, Deontology & Virtue Ethics
Open →
Topics:
  • Utilitarianism: greatest good for greatest number
  • Deontology: Kant's categorical imperative
  • Virtue Ethics: Aristotle's character-based approach
  • Apply each to a data privacy decision at a SaaS company
Deliverables:
  • Framework memo (due Fri Feb 13)
  • Case reflection on Theranos (due Sun Feb 15)
  • Reflection prompt
Week 2
Corporate Governance
Locked
Board composition and director independence, CEO/Chair separation, audit and compensation committees, fiduciary duty to shareholders. How public company boards differ from startup boards — and why that matters when you're raising a Series A. Governance failures: what went wrong at Theranos, WeWork, and FTX at the board level. Deliverables: Problem Set 2, Governance structure comparison memo.
Week 3
Stakeholder Theory vs. Shareholder Primacy
Locked
Milton Friedman's shareholder doctrine vs. R. Edward Freeman's stakeholder theory. The Business Roundtable's 2019 statement redefining corporate purpose. How Okta navigates competing interests: customer data trust, employee wellbeing, investor returns, and community impact. Which stakeholder model is right for your startup? Deliverables: Problem Set 3, Stakeholder map for Okta or your startup concept.
Week 4
Ethics in Tech — Data Privacy, AI Bias & Surveillance
Locked
GDPR, CCPA, and the legal floor vs. the ethical ceiling for identity data. How Okta's zero-trust model is itself an ethical stance on trust and access. AI hiring bias and the limits of algorithmic decision-making. Facial recognition bans and surveillance capitalism. As a future CTO: what does responsible AI deployment look like in practice? Deliverables: Problem Set 4, Ethics audit of an Okta data practice.
Week 5 · Mid-Course Case Due
ESG — Environmental, Social & Governance Reporting
Locked
Materiality assessments, SASB reporting standards, Scope 1/2/3 emissions accounting for software companies. Employee diversity reporting and pay equity. How investors use ESG scores — and why many think they're broken. Mid-course case: evaluate Okta's published ESG disclosures against SASB software standards and identify gaps.
Week 6
Whistleblowing, Scandal Management & Crisis Communication
Locked
Deep dives into Enron, Theranos, and FTX: what ethical failures actually looked like from inside. How to build a speak-up culture where employees feel safe raising concerns before they become scandals. Crisis communication frameworks for executives: what to say, when to say it, and how transparency builds trust under fire. Deliverables: Problem Set 6, Crisis communication plan.
Week 7
Leadership Integrity — Case Studies in Failure & Success
Locked
What distinguishes leaders who fail ethically from those who succeed under pressure? The "tone at the top" effect and how culture cascades from founders and executives. Integrity as a strategic asset — not just a nice-to-have. Case studies: Reed Hastings, Satya Nadella, and Patrick Collison vs. Elizabeth Holmes, Adam Neumann, and Sam Bankman-Fried. Deliverables: Problem Set 7, Leadership integrity self-assessment.
Week 8 · FINAL PROJECT DUE
Your Company's Ethics Charter & Governance Model
Locked
Final Project: Design the complete ethics infrastructure for your startup: articulate core values, define the governance structure (board composition, audit process), draft a responsible AI policy, establish a whistleblower process, and write an advisory board charter. Deliverable: written ethics charter + governance model document. Due: Apr 2, 2027.
