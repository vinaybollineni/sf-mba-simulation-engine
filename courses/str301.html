STR 301 — Competitive Strategy

Quarter 3 · Year 1 · 10 Weeks · Dec 7, 2026 – Feb 12, 2027

STR 301

Competitive Strategy

Upcoming — Q3 Year 1
Go to Dashboard →
Professor's Welcome Note:

Strategy is the discipline that asks: why do some companies win consistently while others with more resources fail? Okta is a perfect strategy case study — it entered a market dominated by Microsoft and Oracle and outmaneuvered both by focusing relentlessly on independence and the identity layer. Understanding WHY that worked — using Porter's Five Forces, competitive positioning, and platform network effects — will change how you see every business decision you make. This is the course that turns engineers into strategists.

Learning Objectives

  • Apply Porter's Five Forces to analyze industry attractiveness and competitive dynamics
  • Build competitive advantage through cost leadership, differentiation, or focus strategies
  • Analyze technology disruption using the Christensen framework
  • Design platform and ecosystem strategy using network effects
  • Develop a full strategic plan for a business unit or new venture

Weekly Syllabus

Week 1 · Starts Dec 7
What Is Strategy? — Rumelt's Good Strategy Framework
Open →
Topics:
  • The difference between goals and strategy
  • Rumelt's "kernel" model: diagnosis, guiding policy, coherent actions
  • Why most "strategic plans" are just goals in disguise
  • Common strategy failures: fluff, dog-eat-dog, template-filling
  • Apply to: what IS Okta's actual strategy?
Deliverables:
  • Problem Set 1
  • Identify Okta's strategy kernel: write its diagnosis, guiding policy, and 3 coherent actions
Week 2
Industry Analysis — Porter's Five Forces
Locked
Threat of new entrants (barriers to entry), bargaining power of suppliers, bargaining power of buyers, threat of substitutes, rivalry among existing competitors. How to score each force. The sixth force (complements — Andy Grove). Apply Five Forces to the identity security industry. Deliverable: Full Five Forces analysis of the identity security market — rate each force (Low/Medium/High) with evidence.
Week 3
Competitive Advantage — Cost Leadership, Differentiation & Focus
Locked
Porter's generic strategies: cost leadership vs. differentiation vs. focus. "Stuck in the middle" trap. Value vs. cost positioning. Operational effectiveness ≠ strategy. Why Amazon chose cost, Apple chose differentiation, and Okta chose a niche focus that became a platform. Deliverable: Position 5 tech companies on a differentiation-cost matrix with evidence.
Week 4
Value Chain Analysis
Locked
Porter's Value Chain: primary activities (inbound logistics, operations, outbound logistics, marketing/sales, service) and support activities (infrastructure, HR, technology, procurement). Value chain as source of competitive advantage. Apply to a SaaS company's value chain vs. traditional software. Deliverable: Value Chain analysis of Okta — identify the 2 activities that create the most competitive advantage.
Week 5 · Mid-Course Project Due
Disruptive Innovation — The Christensen Framework
Locked
Sustaining vs. disruptive innovation, low-end vs. new-market disruption, innovator's dilemma, jobs-to-be-done as disruption signal. Historical tech disruptions: PC vs. mainframe, cloud vs. on-premise, SaaS vs. enterprise software. Is Okta disrupting or sustaining? Mid-Course: Disruption Analysis — identify a technology company at risk of disruption. Diagnose the threat using Christensen's framework. Recommend a strategic response. (4 pages)
Week 6
Platform & Ecosystem Strategy
Locked
Platform vs. pipeline business models, multi-sided networks, platform governance, complementors vs. competitors. Okta Integration Network (OIN) as a platform strategy. Amazon AWS ecosystem. How to design a platform that attracts developers, customers, AND partners simultaneously. Deliverable: Analyze Okta's Integration Network using platform strategy frameworks. Is it a true platform or just an API marketplace?
Week 7
Blue Ocean Strategy — Creating New Market Space
Locked
Kim & Mauborgne's Blue Ocean framework, Strategy Canvas, Four Actions Framework (Eliminate-Reduce-Raise-Create), non-customer analysis. Examples: Cirque du Soleil, Nintendo Wii, Salesforce in 1999. How to find uncontested market space. Deliverable: Apply the Four Actions Framework to a product area you know well at Okta.
Week 8
Corporate Strategy — Diversification, M&A & Portfolio
Locked
Corporate vs. business unit strategy, related vs. unrelated diversification, BCG Growth-Share Matrix, GE-McKinsey Matrix, synergy types (revenue, cost, financial). When diversification destroys value (conglomerates). Okta's acquisition of Auth0 — was it synergistic? Deliverable: Evaluate the Okta-Auth0 acquisition using the BCG Matrix and synergy analysis.
Week 9
Strategy in Tech — Network Effects & Winner-Take-All
Locked
Types of network effects (direct, indirect, data, social), network effect defensibility, winner-take-most vs. winner-take-all, switching costs as a moat, economies of scale in cloud businesses. Why Okta's NRR of 116% is a strategic asset. Commoditization risk for identity. Deliverable: Identify and diagram Okta's network effects. Score their strength (1-5) using NFX Guild framework.
Week 10 · FINAL PROJECT DUE
Full Competitive Strategy Analysis
Locked
Final Deliverable: Comprehensive Competitive Strategy Analysis (12–15 pages). Subject: a company of your choice (can be your startup idea). Must include: (1) Industry Five Forces analysis, (2) Competitive positioning map, (3) Value chain analysis, (4) Network effects / moat assessment, (5) Disruption threat assessment, (6) Strategic recommendations with 3-year roadmap. Due Feb 12, 2027.
