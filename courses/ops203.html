Quarter 2 · Year 1 · 8 Weeks · May 5 – Jun 26, 2027

OPS 203

Operations & Process Management

Upcoming — Q2 Year 1
Go to Week 1 Dashboard →
Professor's Welcome Note:

You run a data platform team — which means you already live in the world of SLAs, on-call rotations, incident response, and capacity planning. Operations management takes those instincts and gives them a rigorous framework that scales beyond engineering. The Toyota Production System, Lean thinking, and the Theory of Constraints apply just as directly to a software delivery pipeline as to a car assembly line. After this course, you'll see every process you touch — from sprint planning to QBRs to vendor negotiations — through the lens of efficiency, capacity, and throughput. That's the operations mindset every executive needs.

Learning Objectives

  • Apply process flow analysis to identify bottlenecks and throughput constraints
  • Use capacity planning frameworks to optimize resource allocation
  • Apply Lean and Six Sigma thinking to eliminate waste in software and business operations
  • Understand supply chain fundamentals and their SaaS equivalents
  • Build an operational improvement plan for a real business process

Weekly Syllabus

Week 1 · Active Now
Process Flow Analysis & Bottlenecks
Open →
Topics:
  • Process flow diagrams
  • Throughput rate & cycle time
  • Little's Law (WIP = throughput × cycle time)
  • Bottleneck analysis, capacity utilization
  • Apply to: software delivery, onboarding, incidents
Deliverables:
  • Problem Set 1
  • Map your team's software delivery process as a flow diagram; identify the bottleneck
Week 2
Capacity Planning & Resource Management
Locked
Capacity vs. demand, peak load planning, queueing theory basics (M/M/1 queue), resource utilization vs. throughput trade-off (utilization paradox). Why running teams at 100% utilization guarantees delays. Apply to: engineering headcount planning, cloud infrastructure sizing. Deliverable: Capacity model for your data platform team for the next quarter.
Week 3
Lean Thinking & Waste Elimination
Locked
Toyota Production System (TPS), 8 wastes (TIMWOODS), value stream mapping, 5S methodology, Kaizen. Lean applied to software: user story waste, context switching, unfinished work, defects. Why the best engineering teams are actually Lean manufacturing teams. Deliverable: Value stream map of ONE process in your team; identify 3 wastes and propose eliminations.
Week 4 · Mid-Course Project Due
Quality Management — Six Sigma & TQM
Locked
Total Quality Management (TQM), Six Sigma DMAIC methodology, statistical process control (SPC), control charts, process capability (Cp, Cpk). Quality in software: test coverage, defect escape rate, MTTR. The cost of poor quality. Mid-Course Project: Six Sigma DMAIC analysis of a recurring incident or quality problem in your engineering org. Present: Define, Measure, Analyze, Improve, Control plan.
Week 5
Supply Chain Fundamentals
Locked
Supply chain design, inventory management (EOQ, safety stock), supplier relationships, bullwhip effect, supply chain risk. SaaS "supply chain": cloud vendor dependencies, third-party API risk, vendor concentration risk. How Okta's multi-cloud strategy reduces supply chain risk. Deliverable: Map Okta's key technology dependencies and assess concentration risk.
Week 6
Agile Operations & OKRs — From Delivery to Business Rhythm
Locked
Agile operations vs. traditional operations management, OKRs as an operations coordination tool, sprint cadence as a process management technique, portfolio-level capacity allocation. How to connect engineering OKRs to company-level operational goals. Deliverable: Write a full OKR set (3 objectives, 9 key results) for your engineering team aligned to a business outcome.
Week 7
SaaS Operational Metrics — SLAs, Uptime & Incident Management
Locked
SLA vs. SLO vs. SLI (Google SRE framework), uptime/availability math (five 9s), MTTR, MTTD, incident severity frameworks, post-mortem process. How to present operational performance metrics to a non-technical executive audience. Deliverable: Build an operational health dashboard (on paper) for your data platform — select 6 metrics, define targets, identify current state vs. target.
Week 8 · FINAL PROJECT DUE
Operational Transformation Plan
Locked
Final Deliverable: Operations Transformation Plan (8–10 pages). Choose a real company (or a business process you know well). Document: (1) Current state process map, (2) Bottleneck and waste analysis, (3) Capacity model, (4) Quality metrics and gap, (5) Proposed future state, (6) Implementation roadmap with 30/60/90 milestones, (7) Expected ROI from improvements. Due Jun 26, 2027.
