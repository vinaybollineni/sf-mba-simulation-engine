PM 602 — Product Management & Innovation

Quarter 6 · Year 2 · 10 Weeks · Nov 3, 2027 – Jan 12, 2028

PM 602

Product Management & Innovation

Upcoming — Quarter 6
Go to Dashboard →
Professor's Welcome Note:

You've been shipping software as an engineering manager. PM 602 is about learning to decide what to ship and why — before a single line of code is written. The best engineering managers become the best product leaders because they can hold both the technical feasibility and the business value in their heads simultaneously. This course gives you the discovery, prioritization, and roadmap skills to cross that threshold — and build products that create real market value.

Learning Objectives

  • Apply continuous discovery habits to uncover customer problems worth solving
  • Use structured frameworks (RICE, ICE, Kano) to prioritize ruthlessly
  • Build a product roadmap that reflects business strategy — not just engineering capacity
  • Define and move a North Star metric through product-led experiments
  • Develop an AI-first product strategy that builds durable competitive advantage

Weekly Syllabus

Week 1 · Active Now
What Great Product Management Looks Like — Outcome Over Output
Open →
Topics:
  • PM vs. EM mental model shift
  • Outcome over output thinking
  • Empowered product teams (Cagan)
  • The "feature factory" failure mode
Deliverables:
  • PM vs. EM self-assessment (due Fri Nov 7)
  • Identify 3 "outcome gaps" in a product you use daily (due Sun Nov 9)
Week 2
Product Discovery — Customer Interviews & Opportunity Mapping
Locked
Teresa Torres' continuous discovery habits; opportunity solution trees; how to run a weekly discovery cadence as a working PM; the difference between what customers want and what they actually need. Practice: run two discovery interviews this week.
Week 3
Prioritization Frameworks — RICE, ICE & MoSCoW
Locked
RICE (Reach, Impact, Confidence, Effort) scoring in practice; when frameworks fail and politics takes over; how to navigate stakeholder pressure in prioritization; the "urgent vs. important" trap that kills most product roadmaps.
Week 4
Product Roadmap Strategy — Now / Next / Later
Locked
Now/next/later vs. timeline roadmaps; why feature roadmaps kill strategic thinking; how to communicate the same roadmap to engineering, design, sales, and executives without losing coherence. Roadmap as a communication tool, not a Gantt chart.
Week 5 · Mid-Project Due
OKRs for Product Teams
Locked
Connecting product metrics to company OKRs; how Okta uses OKRs at the product team level; writing measurable key results for fuzzy product goals. Mid-project due: Full product discovery research report for your startup concept.
Week 6
Pricing & Packaging for Tech Products
Locked
Product-led pricing; usage-based vs. seat-based models; how Okta's pricing evolved from per-user to platform bundles as it scaled; packaging decisions for enterprise vs. SMB segments. The value metric as the foundation of SaaS pricing.
Week 7
Platform Product Strategy — APIs as Products
Locked
APIs as products; developer experience (DX) as a competitive moat; how Okta's developer platform creates defensibility through ecosystem lock-in; building for extensibility vs. building for control. The platform vs. product strategic choice.
Week 8
Product Analytics — Defining & Moving the North Star
Locked
North Star metric selection criteria; funnel instrumentation for product growth; Amplitude/Mixpanel best practices; experiment design and statistical significance for product changes. How to know if your experiment result is real or noise.
Week 9
AI-Powered Product Strategy
Locked
AI features vs. AI-native products; how to integrate LLMs into enterprise products responsibly; identity + AI: access control for AI agents; build vs. buy vs. API decisions for AI capabilities. Where AI creates defensibility and where it commoditizes.
Week 10 · FINAL PROJECT DUE
Product Strategy Document for Your Startup
Locked
Final Project: Full PRD-style product strategy document: problem statement, customer research evidence, solution overview, success metrics, product roadmap (now/next/later), competitive differentiation, and go-to-market tie-in. Due: Jan 12, 2028.
