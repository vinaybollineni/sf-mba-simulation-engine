📖 This Week's Lecture Summary

ACCT 101 · Week 1: The Accounting Equation & Double-Entry Bookkeeping

Professor Note:

As a data engineer, you already understand data models. Think of accounting as a data model for a business. Every transaction is an event that changes the state of the model. The accounting equation is the invariant that must always hold true — much like a database constraint.

The Accounting Equation

Assets = Liabilities + Equity

This equation is the foundation of all accounting. It must always balance. Every financial transaction affects at least two accounts (double-entry) and the equation always remains true.

The Three Components

Assets
Resources owned by the company with future economic value.

Examples: Cash, Accounts Receivable, PP&E, Intangibles
Liabilities
Obligations owed to others — what the company must pay.

Examples: Accounts Payable, Debt, Deferred Revenue
Equity
Residual interest — what's left for shareholders after liabilities.

Examples: Common Stock, Retained Earnings, APIC

Double-Entry Bookkeeping

Every transaction affects at least two accounts. We use debits (left side) and credits (right side). The total debits must always equal total credits.

Example: Okta raises $100M in a VC round
DEBIT: Cash (Asset) +$100,000,000
CREDIT: Common Stock (Equity) +$100,000,000
Equation check: Assets increased by $100M, Equity increased by $100M ✓

Key Takeaways for This Week

  • The accounting equation is immutable — it always balances
  • Every business event is recorded as a transaction affecting ≥2 accounts
  • Assets are what you own; liabilities are what you owe; equity is what's left
  • A balance sheet is a snapshot of the company's financial position at one point in time
  • Deferred revenue (common in SaaS like Okta) is a liability — you've been paid but haven't delivered the service yet
📌 Okta Connection:

When you look at Okta's balance sheet, you'll notice a large "Deferred Revenue" line under liabilities. This is customers who have prepaid for subscriptions but haven't yet received the service. It's one of SaaS accounting's most important concepts — and it directly affects how investors value Okta's business.

📝 Problem Set 1 — Practice Exercises

Record each transaction using the accounting equation. Show the impact on Assets, Liabilities, and Equity.

Q1.

A startup founder invests $50,000 of personal cash into the business. Record this transaction.

Q2.

The company buys $10,000 of office equipment on credit (accounts payable). What is the impact on the accounting equation?

Q3.

A SaaS company collects $120,000 in annual subscription payments upfront from customers. Nothing has been delivered yet. How does this appear on the balance sheet?

Q4.

The company pays $5,000 in salary to employees. What is the accounting entry?

Q5 (Challenge).

Okta raises $1B in a convertible note financing. At the time of issuance, before conversion, how does this appear on the balance sheet? What happens when it converts to equity?

💡 Tip: For each transaction, ask: What went up? What went down? Does Assets = Liabilities + Equity still hold?
